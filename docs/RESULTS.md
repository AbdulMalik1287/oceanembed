# Results — 3-year proof of concept

Train 2019–2020, test 2021 (365 days, held out and scored once). 11,759 wet cells of 24,000.
U-Net 1.93 M parameters; 250 epochs in **357 s**, epoch **144** selected on a validation split that
never touches the test year.

Method in [`METHODOLOGY.md`](METHODOLOGY.md). Raw scores in `~/ocean/runs/*.json` on the GPU box.

## Findings

### 1. Skill concentrates at the thermocline, as hypothesised

Peak improvement is **+22.9% at 100 m with ACC 0.652**, and 75–150 m all sit near +20%. The U-Net
beats the context-free linear baseline by 12% through that band.

This is the result that matters. Those are the depths where the answer cannot be read off the
temperature directly overhead — it must be inferred from mesoscale structure in the surface
pattern, which is exactly what the encoder's receptive field supplies and what the linear model
cannot see. Near the surface the margin is small because SST is an input and the answer is nearly
given.

### 2. There is no real skill below ~300 m

Below 300 m the U-Net matches or slightly trails climatology (−3.1% at 500 m) and ACC decays
towards 0.1.

This is physically honest rather than a defect: daily surface fields carry almost no information
about 500–1000 m. A model claiming skill at 1000 m from surface observations alone should be
treated with suspicion. The useful vertical range is **surface to roughly 200–300 m** — which
covers the mixed layer, the thermocline, and the depths that drive upper-ocean heat content and
cyclone heat potential.

### 3. The residual cold bias is inherited from the climatology, not generated by the model

All models show a cold bias peaking near 100 m. Comparing them isolates the source:

| depth (m) | clim bias | linear bias | unet bias |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 | −0.170 | −0.115 | −0.063 |
| 75 | −0.484 | −0.393 | −0.274 |
| **100** | **−0.628** | −0.523 | **−0.408** |
| 125 | −0.587 | −0.484 | −0.406 |
| 150 | −0.475 | −0.388 | −0.343 |
| 200 | −0.286 | −0.237 | −0.224 |

The reference climatology is itself −0.628 °C off at 100 m: 2021 was warmer at thermocline depths
than the 2019–2020 mean. The U-Net corrects about a third of it. So the bias is a two-year
climatology being too short to represent the seasonal norm, not a modelling failure.

## Operational diagnostics — what this buys a warning centre

None of these can be computed from SST alone; every one needs the vertical profile. Scored on 2021
against GLORYS, over wet cells.

| Diagnostic | RMSE | correlation |
|---|---:|---:|
| **TCHP** — tropical cyclone heat potential | 20.7 kJ cm⁻² | **0.858** |
| D26 — depth of the 26 °C isotherm | 16.7 m | 0.759 |
| MLD — mixed layer depth | 17.3 m | 0.669 |

**TCHP is the one that matters for the Disaster Management theme.** It is the operational predictor
of cyclone rapid intensification in the Bay of Bengal: a deep warm layer keeps feeding a storm even
after its own winds have mixed the surface, whereas a shallow one cools and starves it. Two
cyclones over identical SST can behave completely differently, and the difference is invisible to a
satellite. Reconstructing the profile makes it visible — at 0.86 correlation, daily, basin-wide.

## A methodological note worth keeping

The first attempt at epoch selection held out the **last 15% of training days chronologically**.
That is a contiguous block — September to December 2020 — so the model was validated on a seasonal
regime it had barely trained on. Validation loss bottomed at **epoch 2 of 250**, and the model it
selected was *worse* on the test year than one trained twenty times longer.

Replacing it with **every 5th 10-day block**, so validation spans all seasons, moved the selected
epoch to 144 and produced a sane validation curve. Blocks rather than random days because
consecutive ocean days are near-identical and a random split would leak.

The lesson generalises: with strongly seasonal data, a chronological holdout tests generalisation
across *season* rather than across *time*, and will mislead you about when to stop.

## Next steps, cheapest first

1. **Use the CMEMS long-term monthly climatology**
   (`cmems_mod_glo_phy_my_0.083deg-climatology_P1M-m`, ~375 MB) instead of one computed from two
   years. Should remove most of the inherited bias for free.
2. **More years.** 731 training days is the binding constraint, not compute. The continuous
   all-7-channel record runs 2007–2024; 2010–2024 is ~144 GB and about 12 hours of download.
3. **Report skill only to 300 m**, and say plainly why deeper levels are unskilful.

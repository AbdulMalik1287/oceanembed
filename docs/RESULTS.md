# Results — 3-year proof of concept

Run 2026-09-06. Train 2019–2020 (731 days), test 2021 (365 days, held out and scored once).
11,759 wet cells of 24,000. U-Net trained 40 epochs in **65 seconds** on one MIG slice.

Method in [`METHODOLOGY.md`](METHODOLOGY.md). Raw scores in `~/ocean/runs/*.json` on the GPU box.

## Findings

### 1. Skill concentrates at the thermocline, as hypothesised

Peak improvement is **+21.3% at 100 m with ACC 0.636**, and 75–150 m all sit near +20%. The U-Net
beats the context-free linear baseline by ~10% through that band.

This is the result that matters. Those are the depths where the answer cannot be read off the
temperature directly overhead — it has to be inferred from mesoscale structure in the surface
pattern, which is exactly what the encoder's receptive field provides and what the linear model
cannot see. Near the surface the margin is small because SST is an input and the answer is nearly
given.

### 2. There is no real skill below ~300 m

Below 300 m the U-Net is *slightly worse* than climatology (−1.8% at 500 m, −3.3% at 1000 m) and
ACC decays to 0.06.

This is physically honest rather than a defect: daily surface fields carry almost no information
about 500–1000 m. A model claiming skill at 1000 m from surface observations alone should be
treated with suspicion. The useful vertical range of this approach is **surface to roughly
200–300 m** — which happens to cover the mixed layer, the thermocline, and the depths that drive
upper-ocean heat content and cyclone heat potential.

### 3. The residual cold bias is inherited from the climatology, not generated by the model

All models show a cold bias peaking near 100 m. Comparing them isolates the source:

| depth (m) | clim bias | linear bias | unet bias |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 | −0.170 | −0.115 | −0.115 |
| 75 | −0.484 | −0.393 | −0.400 |
| **100** | **−0.628** | −0.523 | −0.521 |
| 125 | −0.587 | −0.484 | −0.472 |
| 150 | −0.475 | −0.388 | −0.357 |
| 200 | −0.286 | −0.237 | −0.213 |

The reference climatology is itself −0.628 °C off at 100 m: 2021 was warmer at thermocline depths
than the 2019–2020 mean. Both models *partially correct* it, to about −0.52 °C. So the bias is a
two-year climatology being too short to represent the seasonal norm, not a modelling failure.

## Operational diagnostics — what this buys a warning centre

None of these can be computed from SST alone; every one needs the vertical profile. Scored on 2021
against GLORYS, over wet cells.

| Diagnostic | RMSE | correlation |
|---|---:|---:|
| **TCHP** — tropical cyclone heat potential | 20.3 kJ cm⁻² | **0.864** |
| D26 — depth of the 26 °C isotherm | 16.7 m | 0.769 |
| MLD — mixed layer depth | 16.6 m | 0.690 |

**TCHP is the one that matters for the Disaster Management theme.** It is the operational predictor
of cyclone rapid intensification in the Bay of Bengal: a deep warm layer keeps feeding a storm even
after its own winds have mixed the surface, whereas a shallow one cools and starves it. Two
cyclones over identical SST can behave completely differently, and the difference is invisible to a
satellite. Reconstructing the profile makes it visible — at 0.86 correlation, daily, basin-wide.

## Next steps, cheapest first

1. **Use the CMEMS long-term monthly climatology**
   (`cmems_mod_glo_phy_my_0.083deg-climatology_P1M-m`, ~375 MB) instead of one computed from two
   years. Should remove most of the inherited bias for free.
2. **Train longer.** Loss was still falling at epoch 39 (0.1583 → 0.1562). At 65 seconds a run,
   200+ epochs costs nothing.
3. **More years.** 731 training days is the binding constraint, not compute. The continuous
   all-7-channel record runs 2007–2024; 2010–2024 is ~144 GB and about 12 hours of download.
4. **Report skill only to 300 m**, and say plainly why deeper levels are unskilful.
